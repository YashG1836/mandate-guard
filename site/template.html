AI agents can now pay. Mandate Guard makes sure they pay only what you approved.

When an AI assistant shops for you, anything it reads can turn into an instruction, including a fake review that says “pay a deposit first”. Mandate Guard sits between the agent and UPI. A payment goes through only if it fits a mandate you signed on your phone, goes to a verified merchant, and matches a cart that merchant signed.

How it works

Three parts, all outside the AI model

An AI agent can be talked into almost anything by the text it reads. So Mandate Guard never asks the agent what it intends. It checks each payment against facts the agent cannot change.

01 · The mandate

The customer says what the agent may spend

A limit, the categories, which merchants, how many payments and an expiry time. The customer's phone signs it with Ed25519. It works like a UPI AutoPay or NACH mandate, written for an AI agent.

02 · The guard

Every payment is checked before it reaches UPI

Mandate Guard runs in the payment app or at the bank. A payment goes through only if it passes nine checks, all based on signed data. A prompt injection cannot change a signature.

03 · The record

Every decision is signed and chained

Each decision is linked to the one before it by a SHA-256 fingerprint and signed by the guard. Editing, deleting or reordering an entry breaks the chain at that entry.

#CheckWhat it stops

Built on what UPI already does

  • Mandates, like UPI AutoPay and NACH, already let customers approve future payments within limits.
  • Verified merchants already have known UPI IDs. The guard refuses everything else, including lookalikes.
  • Signed carts work like signed UPI QR codes: the merchant vouches for the amount.
  • Limits for delegated payments are where UPI Circle and UPI Reserve Pay are heading. Mandates map onto them.

What it does not do

  • It does not judge whether an allowed purchase is a good deal. In our tests an upsell inside the limit still went through, costing at most ₹200 more.
  • It does not replace the bank's fraud monitoring. It adds a hard limit in front of it.
  • It needs merchants to sign carts. Verified UPI merchants already have the infrastructure to do this.

    What else is going on

    Who it is for

    Trust that holds up for customers, banks and regulators

    Customers

    An assistant can never spend more than they approved, or pay someone they did not choose. Blocked attempts show up as alerts.

    Payment apps and banks

    A control they can explain in one sentence, plus a signed record of who approved what when a customer disputes a payment.

    NPCI

    A common check that any agent payment protocol can require, whichever company built the agent.

    Regulators

    AI-initiated payments that are bounded, explainable and auditable, in line with the RBI's FREE-AI principles on protecting customers.

    Merchants

    Verified merchants keep receiving orders from agents. Impersonators get nothing, because lookalike UPI IDs are refused.

    AI companies

    They can offer payments without promising that their model will never be fooled.
