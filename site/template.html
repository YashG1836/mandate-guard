AI agents can now pay. Mandate Guard makes sure they pay only what you approved.

AI assistants can now shop and pay for you. But they can be tricked by what they read, like a fake review that says “pay a deposit first”. Mandate Guard checks every payment before it reaches UPI, and lets through only what you approved: the right shop, the right amount, nothing extra.

    How it works

    How it works, in three steps

    An AI assistant can be tricked by what it reads. So Mandate Guard never trusts the assistant. It checks each payment against things the assistant cannot change.

    1 · You approve

    You decide what the assistant may spend

    How much, on what, at which shops, how many payments and until when. Your phone signs it digitally, like a UPI AutoPay mandate.

    2 · It checks

    Every payment is checked before it reaches UPI

    Nine quick checks: is the shop real, is the amount right, is it within your limit, and more. A trick hidden in a web page cannot change a digital signature.

    3 · It records

    Every decision is saved in a tamper-proof record

    Each entry is linked to the one before it, so any change shows up. Every decision also comes with a signed receipt anyone can check by scanning it, so disputes are settled from facts.

    The nine checks

    #CheckWhat it stops

    Built on what UPI already does

    • Mandates, like UPI AutoPay and NACH, already let customers approve future payments within limits.
    • Verified merchants already have known UPI IDs. The guard refuses everything else, including lookalikes.
    • Signed carts work like signed UPI QR codes: the merchant vouches for the amount.
    • Limits for delegated payments are where UPI Circle and UPI Reserve Pay are heading. Mandates map onto them.

    What it does not do

    • It does not judge whether an allowed purchase is a good deal. In our tests an upsell inside the limit still went through, costing at most ₹200 more.
    • It does not replace the bank's fraud monitoring. It adds a hard limit in front of it.
    • It needs merchants to sign carts. Verified UPI merchants already have the infrastructure to do this.

    How we measured

      Who it is for

      Trust that holds up for customers, banks and regulators

      Customers

      The assistant can never spend more than you approved, or pay someone you did not choose.

      Payment apps and banks

      A simple safety check, and proof of what the customer approved if there is a dispute.

      NPCI

      One standard check for every AI assistant, whichever company built it.

      Regulators

      AI payments that stay within what customers agreed to and can be audited, in line with the RBI's FREE-AI principles.

      Merchants

      Real shops keep getting orders. Fake lookalike UPI IDs get nothing.

      AI companies

      They can offer payments without promising their AI will never be fooled.

      Plan

      From prototype to pilot

      Now

      Working prototype

      Mandate signing, the nine checks, the signed record and receipts, 70 test scenarios and this demo, tested with a real open-source AI model (Qwen3 8B). Open source.

      December 2026

      More AI models and harder attacks

      Run the benchmark on more AI models, including Claude and larger open models, add multi-step and image-based attacks, and test the mandate screen with 20 people.

      January–February 2027

      Fit UPI's own building blocks

      Map mandates onto UPI Reserve Pay and UPI Circle limits, look up agents in a registry, and add a post-quantum signature option.

      From March 2027

      Sandbox pilot

      Pilot with a payment app or bank through the RBI regulatory sandbox, and test cross-border agent payments through the IFSCA FinTech sandbox at GIFT City.

      What would help us most

      • A payment app or bank willing to run a sandbox pilot.
      • Mentors who know UPI rules and payments regulation.
      • Access to UPI sandbox APIs for agent payments.
